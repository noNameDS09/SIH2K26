# Visual Direction

## North star

Create a living, premium craft catalog: the product is the hero, verification is visible, and the interface feels human without becoming rustic, noisy, or folkloric.

The intended order of feeling is:

1. Attractive
2. Trustworthy
3. Human
4. Premium
5. Energetic

## Public experience

The public site should feel like a carefully curated collection rather than a generic ecommerce template.

- Lead with real product imagery and a clear `View full details` action.
- Use craft references to establish materiality and orientation, not to decorate every empty space.
- Keep the catalog understandable in a few seconds.
- Show authenticity and verification as evidence, not as marketing badges.
- Keep supporting story sections short and subordinate to product discovery.

## Artisan experience

The artisan workflow should feel like a calm guided workshop, not an administration dashboard.

- One primary action per screen.
- Short sentences and familiar labels.
- Voice input is first-class in the artisan workflow.
- Always show a visual review and confirmation step after voice capture.
- Use icons together with text; never rely on a symbol alone.
- Make progress, recovery, and completion obvious.

## Visual character

Use editorial composition, tactile spacing, confident typography, and restrained material cues. Expressiveness should come from scale, rhythm, image treatment, and purposeful motion—not from decorative clutter.

## Absolute anti-goals

- No orange in the web design system.
- No visible AI branding, provider names, chatbot language, robot imagery, or synthetic-looking people/products.
- No generic SaaS dashboard treatment.
- No glassmorphism, random gradients, neon glows, or decorative blobs.
- No excessive pill controls or every-element-in-a-card layouts.
- No star ratings, invented social proof, or unsupported claims.
- No autoplay audio or public-catalog voice assistant.
- No product image buried under patterns, overlays, or ornamental UI.
